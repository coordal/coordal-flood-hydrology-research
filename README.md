# Coordal Flood & Hydrology Research

Research and development workspace for Coordal Technologies Ltd.'s
flood modelling, terrain analysis, hydrological flow prediction,
and geospatial data engineering initiatives.

## 1. Research Programme

The programme investigates how geospatial datasets, terrain analysis,
hydrological modelling, and infrastructure data can be combined to
develop reproducible flood-assessment workflows for selected areas
in Ghana.

The research progresses from data preparation and terrain processing
to flow analysis, catchment delineation, rainfall-runoff modelling,
flood-inundation assessment, and infrastructure exposure analysis.

## 2. Research Objectives

- Inventory and validate relevant geospatial datasets.
- Develop reproducible geospatial data-processing pipelines.
- Process digital elevation models and terrain representations.
- Investigate flow direction, flow accumulation, and drainage networks.
- Delineate catchments and watersheds.
- Evaluate rainfall-runoff modelling methods.
- Investigate flood-inundation modelling approaches.
- Assess potential exposure of buildings, roads, and other infrastructure.
- Document research methods, results, assumptions, and limitations.
- Prepare validated workflows for potential integration into Coordal's
  geospatial platform.

## 3. Research Workflow

Research work is organised through the following stages:

1. Research Backlog
2. Hypothesis / Design
3. Data Preparation
4. Experiment
5. Validation
6. Implementation
7. Documented

The Kanban board is the authoritative tracker for assignments,
priorities, progress, and deliverables.

## 4. Technical Environment

The research may use the following technologies, subject to approval
and project requirements:

- Python
- PostgreSQL and PostGIS
- GeoPandas and Shapely
- Rasterio and NumPy
- Appropriate terrain and hydrological analysis libraries
- Approved GIS tools
- Git and GitHub

The specific tools and dependencies for each workflow must be
documented.

## 5. Research Principles

All research should follow these principles:

- Reproducible data processing and experimentation.
- Documented data provenance and processing parameters.
- Appropriate validation and quality assurance.
- Explicit treatment of assumptions and uncertainty.
- Clear distinction between exploratory outputs and validated results.
- Secure handling of Company information and datasets.
- Maintainable code and technical documentation.

## 6. Repository Structure

The repository will evolve as the research progresses.

Suggested initial structure:

- `docs/` — Research documentation and methodology.
- `src/` — Reusable geospatial processing code.
- `tests/` — Automated tests and validation checks.
- `configs/` — Approved processing configurations.
- `scripts/` — Executable research workflows.
- `notebooks/` — Approved exploratory analysis.
- `README.md` — Repository overview and setup instructions.

Large datasets, credentials, and confidential materials must not be
committed without explicit authorisation.

## 7. Project Management

Research tasks and milestones are tracked through the
**Coordal — Flood & Hydrology Research** Kanban project.

Each substantive task should document its objective, inputs,
methodology, expected outputs, validation requirements, and
relevant limitations.

## 8. Security and Data Handling

All work must comply with Coordal's applicable confidentiality,
intellectual property, information security, and data protection
requirements.

Use approved Company systems and repositories. Do not commit
credentials, API keys, personal data, or unauthorised datasets.

## 9. Ownership and Contributions

Contributions must comply with the applicable agreements governing
the research engagement and intellectual property.

Research outputs must be reviewed and documented before being
treated as validated or suitable for operational use.
